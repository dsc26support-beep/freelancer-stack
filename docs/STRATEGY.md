# Freelancer Stack: strategy

**Goal:** an honest comparison site for freelancers choosing business software (invoicing, contracts/CRM, time tracking, scheduling). Revenue from affiliate commissions disclosed on every page.

**Channel:** SEO articles first, plus an email list (free invoice template). Static site (Astro) deployed from GitHub.

## Affiliate programs: research status (checked 2026-11-06)

Terms change without notice. **Confirm every number on the program's own page after you are approved.** Third-party directories disagree with each other, so treat them as leads only.

| Program | What I found | Confidence | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| FreshBooks | Directories say about $10 per free trial and up to $200 per paid plan, with a 120-day cookie. They disagree on which network runs it (Impact, ShareASale, PartnerStack, FlexOffers all appear). | Medium | Apply via FreshBooks' own affiliate page and read the terms you are given. |
| HoneyBook | Its own affiliate fee page (revised Aug 2025) says $50 per qualified referral that stays subscribed 100 days. Referral revenue is capped at $10,000 unless you are in its Educator program. | High | Apply on honeybook.com. Note the 100-day wait before payment. |
| Dubsado | Its own pages describe subscription credit (about $35) for referrals, not cash. One directory claims 20% recurring, which the official pages do not support. | Medium-high that it is credit, not cash | Poor fit as a main earner. Mention honestly if you cover it. |
| Calendly | One directory says Calendly has no public affiliate program. Other directories list a 15% recurring rate via PartnerStack. They conflict. | Low | Check PartnerStack directly. Do not build articles around Calendly commissions until confirmed. |
| Harvest | Nothing reliable found. | None | Check Harvest's site and PartnerStack/Impact directly. |

**Takeaway:** the numbers are smaller and less certain than directories suggest. HoneyBook and FreshBooks are the most credible cash programs. Plan for modest income.

## Rules for this site

1. Every article has a disclosure at the top and `rel="sponsored"` on affiliate links.
2. No claim about price, features, or earnings unless it is checked against the vendor's own page on the date of writing.
3. No "I tested this" wording unless the author actually did. Draft articles stay `draft: true` until the author adds first-hand notes.
4. Say who each tool is *not* for.
5. Do not promise income. Do not fake urgency.
